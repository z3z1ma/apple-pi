# Due diligence

Make the cost and consequences of ownership visible before commitment. Read and follow the [shared process conventions](../handbook-writing/references/processes.md).

The prospective owner chairs the inquiry. Use `technical-due-diligence-partner` for hidden commitments, `staff-security-engineer` for trust and data, `infrastructure-cfo` for cost, and `maintainer-from-three-years-ahead` for continuing burden. Add legal or other expertise when the actual question requires it; the current catalog does not create missing professional authority.

1. **Name the commitment.** Identify the exact option/version, intended use, alternatives, affected data and what would become costly to undo. Distinguish a reversible evaluation from adoption.
2. **Investigate independently.** Give each needed perspective its question. Follow decisive claims to primary terms, source, observable behavior or an authorized trial. Separate facts, estimates, missing evidence and matters requiring qualified advice.
3. **Reconcile the liabilities.** Share results in one thread. Check inconsistent assumptions about scope, ownership, maintenance, pricing, access, migration and exit. A supplier's promise is not verification of the receiving system.
4. **Return decision-ready evidence.** Record obligations, exit/recovery options, unresolved questions and the checks that could settle them. Use [decision-review](../decision-review/SKILL.md) when the choice needs deliberation.

Done is a usable basis for the authorized owner to decide. This process neither signs terms nor treats absent evidence as an acceptable risk on that owner's behalf.
