# Running an organizational process

Choose a process when the exchange between people can change the result. A direct question or one capable owner is often enough. Work within the current goal and authority; selecting a skill grants no new permission.

## Put responsibility before ceremony

Name the person accountable for the result and who may make the decision. A chair coordinates the exchange; affected owners retain their responsibilities. Ask the operator at a real authority boundary using [operator-sign-off](../../operator-sign-off/SKILL.md), rather than requiring a vote on every within-scope judgment.

Find standing people with relevant experience. Confirm current execution or availability before relying on a contribution; idle status alone is not spare capacity. Use your manager tools, or ask the responsible manager for people and commitments. A process invitation is not an assignment. Choose the perspectives the case needs, not a fixed board or every role in the catalog.

## Give the exchange one useful home

Keep work discussion in its task thread. Use group mail and `reply` when people must answer each other's positions; a contributor's separate assignment thread does not reach the whole group. Use an existing topic channel for context that should remain available, with explicit attention when a response is needed. Let peers settle ordinary questions directly.

Retain the current owner, decision, next action, evidence and unresolved questions in the governing task's ordinary artifacts. Pin a short current-context message when it helps people join or resume. Reuse existing records rather than create another workflow state store.

## Recover and conclude honestly

When a contributor is unavailable, distinguish missing critical input from an optional view. Seek an authorized replacement, narrow the result or report the blocker; make coverage limits visible. A reply, agreement and completed work are different facts.

End with the process's observable result, what was verified and what remains uncertain. A receiver confirms a handoff; until then, keep responsibility explicit. Finish or reassign actual work separately from closing its discussion. Return borrowed capacity through its manager; closing a process is not permission to delete people.

A multi-person process closes with a short exchange on what helped or hindered it, feeding the task's retrospective. Use a deeper retrospective only when the experience warrants it. Keep durable follow-up in an owned task; reminders are session-local, not durable scheduling.
