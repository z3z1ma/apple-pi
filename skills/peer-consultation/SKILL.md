# Peer consultation

Get the owner to a useful next move, not a collection of disconnected opinions. Read and follow the [shared process conventions](../handbook-writing/references/processes.md).

The problem owner chairs. Choose peers for the missing perspectives: `principal-engineer` for feasibility, `paradigm-outsider` for reframing, or the relevant domain specialist. A consequential disputed choice belongs in [decision review](../decision-review/SKILL.md).

1. **Frame the help.** The owner states the problem, constraints, attempts and what help would change. Peers ask clarifying questions before offering solutions; proceed when they can restate the actual obstacle.
2. **Let peers reason together.** In one group-mail thread, peers develop and challenge alternatives while the owner holds back rebuttals. Ask for a discriminating observation where advice depends on an uncertain claim. The owner answers new factual questions without defending a favorite solution.
3. **Return the choice.** The owner says what changed their view, chooses the next action and states how to check it. Investigation or implementation that follows gets an explicit owner and the required authority.

Done is an owned next move with a checkable prediction, or a clearly named missing fact or decision. Keep advice distinct from something tested. A consultation need not become a project or a standing group.
