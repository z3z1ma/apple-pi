# Retrospective

Learn something that changes future action without inventing a tidy causal story. Read and follow the [shared process conventions](../handbook-writing/references/processes.md).

Include people who did and received the work. A `coordinator` can facilitate; choose another facilitator when the owner's decisions are central. Use domain expertise for disputed explanations and `technical-writer` when the lasting record needs care.

1. **Collect observations first.** Participants independently identify consequential events and evidence, including what worked. Build a shared account that labels missing or conflicting facts before debating explanations.
2. **Test the explanations.** Discuss what the observations support, what they merely suggest and which counterexample would change the account. A successful rerun does not explain the earlier failure. Preserve uncertainty when the cause was not established.
3. **Choose useful changes.** Agree improvements, retained practices or a reason to make no change. Give accepted follow-up an actual owner and a way to check its effect; participants confirm their own commitments.
4. **Keep the right record.** Put task-specific learning in its retrospective. Promote reusable knowledge to the existing documentation, wiki or guidance owner under that owner's review rules. Record future checks as promises until the results exist.

Done is a concise evidence-qualified account and accepted follow-up, not a transcript or a list of aspirations. The short closeout of this retrospective stays short; it does not start another retrospective.
