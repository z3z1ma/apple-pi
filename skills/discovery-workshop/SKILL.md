# Discovery workshop

Find an evidence-backed problem worth pursuing, or the inquiry that can settle it. Read and follow the [shared process conventions](../handbook-writing/references/processes.md).

A `product-manager` owns the outcome; a `research-lead` can lead the inquiry. Include `principal-ux-researcher`, `end-user-customer-advocate` and people with actual relevant evidence. Use `paradigm-outsider` when the proposed solution has narrowed the problem prematurely.

1. **Separate observations from the pitch.** Gather examples of users, their current behavior, alternatives and consequences. Participants first record their own observations and uncertainties before seeing the favored framing. Agent opinions and advocacy are not customer evidence.
2. **Compare problem frames.** In one shared discussion, ask what each frame explains, what contradicts it and which unknown changes the decision. Include the possibility that the problem does not justify new software.
3. **Choose the next evidence.** Use [research](../research/SKILL.md) for source work, [prototype](../prototype/SKILL.md) for a design question, or [experiment-cycle](../experiment-cycle/SKILL.md) for a discriminating observation. External contact and data collection need their own authority.
4. **Return the finding.** The product owner records the supported problem and intended outcome, chooses an owned inquiry, or stops. Keep unresolved needs and limits beside the conclusion.

Done is a defensible problem statement or a concrete next inquiry with its decision consequence. It is not a feature list or simulated customer consensus.
