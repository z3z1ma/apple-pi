# Operating review

Improve a concrete operating condition, not a dashboard's appearance. Read and follow the [shared process conventions](../handbook-writing/references/processes.md).

The manager accountable for the work chairs with the affected owners. Choose a specialist from the suspected constraint: performance, cost, simplicity, reliability or evidence. A person without inspection or staffing capabilities asks the responsible manager to supply that view or action.

1. **Choose the question and window.** Use `progress` for work outcomes, `bottlenecks` for explicit dependencies, `coordination` for discussion beside work, and `experience` for relevant exposure. Follow aggregate evidence into specific tasks, links and conversations. Reuse a cutoff when comparing detail pages.
2. **Ask what the records miss.** Talk directly to the affected people. Separate recorded conditions from causal hypotheses. High discussion may support delivery; idle status may conceal commitments. A task reached through two dependency paths counts once as an affected task, while both relationships still matter.
3. **Choose the smallest useful response.** Make an authorized change, run a discriminating observation, or keep the arrangement stable. State the expected effect and what would disprove the explanation. Keep `economics` and `predictions` optional, with comparable premises when used.
4. **Own the follow-up.** Record the decision, evidence, remaining uncertainty and who checks the result. Reinspect the same relevant condition; a before/after change alone does not prove causation. A timer can remind its owner, but the task retains the commitment across session changes.

Done is a justified operating decision and an owned check, including a reasoned no-change result. Distinguish a proposed improvement from one observed later.
