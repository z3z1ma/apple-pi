# Theme packs

A theme pack is one directory here, named by the theme's id. The world draws the same places, characters, states, and actions in every pack; a pack changes only how they look and what they are called. A pack is a set of files, not code: its manifest, a stylesheet for the world's HTML, paintings with their place data, character sheets and portraits, and overlays. The contract and the pack check live in `../src/theme-pack.ts`; this page is the summary for theme authors.

Check a pack before you open it in the world, from the repository's root:

```sh
node --import ./components/session-state/src/register.mjs apps/agent-world/src/check-theme.ts apps/agent-world/themes/<id>
```

It prints each problem as `<pack>/<file>: <fault>` and exits 1 when there is any. Without a directory it checks every pack here. The world checks a pack again before it wears it; a broken pack never half-loads: the world stays in (or opens in) the last good theme and says on screen which file is wrong and why.

## Layout

| Path | What |
| --- | --- |
| `theme.json` | The manifest (below). |
| `interface/skin.css` | How the world's HTML looks (the manifest's `skin`), with any textures it uses beside it. |
| `places/<place>.json` | Each place's data over its painting `places/<painting>.png`. |
| `characters/looks.json` | Every look: its role, frame size, and baseline. |
| `characters/<look>.png` | Its 16-frame sheet. |
| `characters/<look>-idle.png` | Optional: its eight idle poses (look left, look right, stretch, wave, laugh, shrug, nod, yawn), named by its `idle` entry in `looks.json`. |
| `characters/<look>-portrait-0..3.png` | Its four expressions: neutral, happy, thinking, worried. |
| `overlays/<name>.png` | Doors, banners, the bed, letters, birds, fire, water, fidget objects, the cursor, and the click markers. |

## The manifest

- `id` (the directory's name) and `name` (shown in Settings); `description`, one line for Settings.
- `labels`: what the theme calls every place (`headquarters`, `common-room`, `gallery`, `wing`, `square`, `street`, `forge`, `quest-office`, `library`, `inn`, `rookery`) and every role (`coordinator`, `worker`, `independent`, `innkeeper`, `rookery-keeper`, `smith`, `clerk`, `librarian`, `crier`, `townsfolk`, `recruiter`): Guild hall, Office floor, Classroom; Guildmaster, Lead, Class rep. `wing` follows a repository's name ("apple-pi wing").
- `text`: the theme's own lines, by key (`TEXT_KEYS`): what the inn's host says, how mail is named, what the conversation panel says while it opens. `{name}`, `{count}`, and the like are filled in.
- `style`: CSS custom properties put on the page's root (`STYLE_TOKENS`): the fonts, the inks, the accent, the danger colour, the backdrop, and the main surfaces of the interface frame and the conversation panel (`--aw-frame-background`, `--aw-surface-background`, `--aw-panel-cover`, `--aw-panel-page`, `--aw-veil`). A value may name a file in the pack with `url(...)`, relative to the pack (frame textures). Optional `--aw-icon-<tab>` tokens (`TAB_ICONS`: `agents`, `needs-you`, `work`, `mail`, `slack`, `places`, `settings`) give a side-panel tab the pack's own icon, `url(...)` of an SVG in the pack drawn in the tab's ink through a mask (only its shape counts, so draw it in any one colour on transparency): fantasy's scroll and signpost, the tech office's board and pin, the anime school's notebook and school. A tab without one wears the world's own.
- `skin`: the stylesheet, relative to the pack.
- `ambient` (optional): the pack's cast of ambient characters, there only for fun and immersion, by cast name (lowercase words joined by `-`): each a `name` (hover text and the menu), a `look` whose role is `ambient`, an `examine` line, at least three `lines` it says in turn when talked to (in the small dialogue box), one or more `reactions` it says instead when clicked again and again, optional `barks` it says to nobody now and then in a speech bubble (short: one or two lines of a bubble), and an optional `gesture`, the idle it plays when it reacts (`look-around`, `stretch`, `wave`, `laugh`, `shrug`, `nod`, `yawn`; default a shrug). Each member stands in exactly one place (a place's `ambient`, below). It sends no command and makes no outside call. A member marked `crew: true` is the headquarters crew, the staff of every headquarters: it stands in `headquarters` or `common-room`, and since the world shows those once per coordinator, in every coordinator's copy. The crew may wear a `townsfolk` look as well as an `ambient` one, never a look the rest of the cast wears; the built-in packs staff the hall with four and the common room with two.
- `world`: what the world draws on its canvas, where CSS does not reach: the backdrop, the font, speech bubbles, the small sign, sleepers' "z", state badges, the colour of lamp glow, where a banner's name goes (`banner.text` and `banner.hover`, fractions of `banner-cream.png`), and, when a place has a `bed` slot, where a sleeper's head and the covers are in `bed.png` (px); and, when the notice sprites paint board around the paper, which part of them is the notice (`notice.paper`, fractions) and whether to cut the board left around it away (`notice.cutOut`); and how the square's building signs are lettered (`sign`: a `font`, an `ink`, an optional `shadow`, `upper` for capitals, and the `plate` colours `fill`, `rim`, and `edge` for boards the world draws).

## The skin

The world UI's own CSS only lays things out. The skin gives them their look: every colour, face, border, background, and shadow, for the classes the world's HTML uses. It loads after the world's CSS, so at equal specificity its rules win, and it may change layout too where a theme needs it (a document window's title bar, a notebook's binding). The check makes sure it styles at least the classes in `SKIN_CLASSES` (the interface's frames, surfaces, tabs, and buttons; the conversation panel and its panes; the places' panels; the pointer's menu). `fantasy/interface/skin.css` is the full reference: an open book; `tech-office` draws a document window, `anime-school` a notebook.

## Shared conventions

Shared by every pack, so the world works the same in each:

- **The places** above, each with its painting and data file (`PlaceData`): a grid of 48 px tiles traced on a half-tile grid, blocked rects, spots, entries, doors (rect, stand point, destination, and in the square the building's sign board, `sign.rect`, where the place's name is lettered; `sign.plate` when the painting has no board fit to letter and the world draws one), overlay slots, and the place's host. The square's host is its crier (a `crier` look), whom the operator asks for any Slack channel; its spots hold independent agents first and, on those left free, one townsperson per Slack channel with unread messages, so the spots are also how many townsfolk the square holds. The `street` is the second outside place, an open-air painting (the harbour lane, the main street, the shopping street) joined to the square by an exit on an open edge: a square door whose `to` is `street` (no `sign`; the minimap marks it with an arrow on its edge and the label as "To …") and, in the street, a door back whose `to` is `square` and is its `enterBy`. Both places name an entry for the other (`entries.street`, `entries.square`). The street's spots hold the townsfolk the square has no free spot for, in the same order. The forge's host is its smith (a `smith` look), and its `work` slots, on the workbenches, are where the open pull requests lie (the rest wait "on the rack", in the smith's list): the check requires both. The quest office's host is its clerk (a `clerk` look), and its `notice` slots, on its board, are where the operator's Jira issues are pinned, the most pressing first (the rest wait "at the counter", in the clerk's list); the library's host is its librarian (a `librarian` look). A `furniture` piece whose `use` is `clerk` or `librarian` opens that host's list (the piece holding the notices reads as the board). Recruiting new agents happens in two places: the headquarters' `furniture` piece whose `use` is `recruit` (the coordinator's desk) recruits a worker who reports to that coordinator, and the square's `recruiter` (its feet at `at`, on open floor in sight, at a booth) recruits an independent agent; the recruiter wears its `look` when given, else the pack's first `recruiter` look, else a townsperson's. The manifest names them in the theme's words: the `recruiter` label and the `recruit-*` lines (the guild recruits, the office hires, the club invites). A place's `interest` points (each a `name`, its feet at `at`, and the way it `face`s: a stall, the fountain, a bench, a window) are where those standing about stroll to and pause: townsfolk and idle agents, never those at work. Each is on open floor, a tile clear of every door's stand point and entry, and clear of every spot, the host, and the recruiter. A place's `ambient` entries stand the manifest's cast members there: each names one (`cast`), its feet at `at` on open floor, a tile clear of doors' stand points and entries and clear of spots, the host, the recruiter, the points of interest, and the rest of the cast, facing `face` (else the room); it strolls to the place's points of interest unless it `stay`s at its post (the guard by the guild hall's doors). Keep the cast out of rooms that repeat (headquarters, common rooms, wings), as pets are, except the headquarters crew (`crew`), who belong in every headquarters and its common room. No two cast members in one place wear the same look. Geometry is not shared: each theme lays out its own rooms. The painting must fit the grid (the same px per tile across and down); every spot, entry, and door's stand point must be on open floor.
- **Character sheets**: 16 frames in one row (walking down, up, and left, four phases each; seated twice; standing; asking), every frame standing on the look's baseline, 120 px from head to feet standing (frame 14). Four portraits each, 360 px tall. An idle sheet, when a look has one, is eight standing poses in a row on its own frame size and baseline (`idle` in `looks.json`), the same person as the sheet: someone standing about plays them now and then.
- **Overlays**: the slot kinds (`fire`, `water`, `glow`, `banner`, `fidget`, `bed`, `window`, `letters`, `work`, `notice`) and the overlay names. A `notice` slot is a notice's centre on the board (`at`) and its width (`w`), small enough that board shows between the notices and around the board's edge; its notices are `notice-open`, `notice-urgent` (a high priority), `notice-progress` (work started), and `notice-done`. A `work` slot gives the bottom centre of what a piece covers (`at`), its width (`w`), and for a diagonal piece such as a blade a clockwise `turn` in degrees that lays it flatter along its bench; its pieces are `blade-passing`, `blade-failing`, and `blade-running` (whatever the theme draws: blades, devices, robots).
- **Pets**: a `pet` fidget wears the kind's own sprites (`pet-0..3`) or, with a slot's `look`, one of the pack's other pets (`pet-<look>-0..3`), each named in `theme.json`'s `fidgets.pet.looks` with its own `name`, `examine` line, and optionally its own `verbs`, `particle`, `height`, and `frames`. `height` is how tall it sits beside a standing character, as a share of the character's height (a cat about 0.33, a dog about 0.5, an owl, a hamster, or a hedgehog less), and sets its size whatever size it was painted. `frames` says what each of its four sprites shows, in order, from `sit`, `petted` (eyes closed), `play`, `stretch`, and `sleep` (default: sit, petted, play, sleep). A place holds at most one pet, and no two places share a pet's look (a `sleeper` counts as a pet); a slot with `asleep` rests curled up on its `sleep` sprite, purrs on when petted, and wakes to play. Keep pets out of rooms that repeat (headquarters, common rooms, wings), where one look would show in each copy.
- **Atmosphere**: a place's `atmosphere` lists the motion the world draws in code over its painting, not characters: `motes` (dust in the light, over `rect`), `fall` (`what`: `leaves` or `petals`), `sky` (`what`: `birds` or a `plane`, crossing now and then), `steam` (from `at`, `w` tiles wide), `sparks` (embers rising from a fire at `at`), `blink` (small lights over `rect`, in `colors`), `screens` (each screen in `rects` glowing and flickering), `shimmer` (glints where the painting inside `rect` is water), and, out of doors only, `lights` (the painted windows and lamps inside `rects`, lit at evening and night). Every place names at least one moving effect; the square and the street name a `sky` and their `lights` (and, in the fantasy and school packs, a `fall`). Out of doors the world tints the painting by the local time of day (morning, afternoon, golden hour, evening, night; `web/game/daylight.ts`), and lamps glow brighter at night. Particles stay small and few, in the pack's palette (STYLE.md).
- **The pointer**: every pack draws the world's cursor as a hand in its style, pointing up and to the left: `cursor-point` (the plain hand, over open floor), `cursor-tap` (the finger pressed down, alternated with `cursor-point` while the pointer is over something to do), and `cursor-blocked` (a fist or a greyed hand, over floor nobody can walk to). The world scales them to a cursor about 30 px tall, at the scale of `cursor-point`, with the hotspot at the fingertip: the top-left-most pixel of the sprite's largest opaque shape, so sparkles drawn beside the tapping hand do not move it. `mark-walk` (a yellow X) and `mark-act` (a red X) are the click markers, drawn about 22 px wide where a click sends the avatar and on what a click acts on; each fades within half a second.

## Required and optional

Required, or the check fails: the manifest with every label, line, and token; the skin; every place's data and painting (both wing sizes); at least three agent looks, a coordinator, an avatar, a townsperson, and each host's look, each with its sheet and four portraits; doors open, closed, and boarded; `banner-cream`; four `bird` frames; and wherever a place has such a slot, four frames of fire or water, `bed` (with `world.bed`), `letters`, the three `blade-*` pieces for `work`, or the four `notice-*` notices. Places with their own needs (`PLACE_NEEDS`: the forge's smith and `work` slots, the quest office's clerk and `notice` slots, the library's librarian, the square's recruiter, the headquarters' recruiting desk; each host wearing a look of its role) fail the check without them. Everything else is optional and simply absent when missing: idle sheets and points of interest (checked when present), extra room sizes, ambient animation, fidget objects, other banners.
