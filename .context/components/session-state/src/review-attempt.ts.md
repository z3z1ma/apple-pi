This is an ordinary program-peer client owned by the runtime, not another model runner. Persist attempt, program, person and command identities before launch; the runtime derives a review scope from that stored program ID. The scope's generation, plan revision, owner, person and role must still match at launch. The review-specific scope checks are separate from ordinary peer-tool scopes.

Save a terminal turn's structured output, observed usage and failure before rejecting cancellation or beginning cleanup. Keep the raw result distinct from validated output, and keep both distinct from completed cleanup. A malformed report can come from a completed model turn that incurred usage. A closed socket cannot prove disposal; scope disappearance under the existing guarded lifecycle does. Preserve partial results when cleanup fails.

Resolve current role/profile policy and compare its fingerprint with the planner catalog before either reuse lookup or new launch. A missing catalog entry also refuses execution. Per-attempt startup/completion checks alone allowed policy A during planning, B during a focused turn and restored A at job completion; B was recorded evidence, not an unobserved edit. Bind accepted components to their planned policy instead of adding a watcher. A resumed job resolves current role/profile policy before reuse lookup. Exact input identity includes that policy, the goal, bound context and output schema; callers additionally reject incomplete coverage. Reuse stays within the same job/plan, except the explicitly selected planner that produced the current plan is eligible across its own plan-revision increment. A new planner result advances plan revision, which prevents inspections under the old plan from receiving current coverage credit. No current attempt writes `reuse_blocked_by`; legacy validator challenges stay where they were recorded and still block reuse. A fresh result is first checked against the attempt's own tool schema, then passes through `fromTool` (the per-attempt tool shape to the stored shape) before `validate`, so converters only see well-formed input; a reused stored output goes to `validate` directly. `thinking`, when set, is sent with set_thinking after start, recorded as the attempt's thinking, and is part of the input key.

Throw `ReviewStop` for anything a retry cannot fix or must not risk: a policy that cannot be resolved (`rolePolicy` wraps the resolver), differs from the planned catalog or changes during startup or the turn, a model outside the recorded policy, lost ownership, damaged captured source (wrapped by `verified`) and unfinished cleanup. A surviving program scope makes the failure a `ReviewStop` even when another error came first, such as a failed program opening whose own cleanup failed. The job retries every other failure once; abort errors keep their identity so cancellation stays cancellation.
Why: retrying a policy or ownership failure launches another paid reviewer that fails the same way, and retrying before cleanup finishes could leave two reviewers holding resources. Return the actual reused or new attempt ID alongside its validated output so the job can record its current evidence explicitly. A new attempt gets new person/program/command identities. Retaining an old result is not permission for an obsolete attempt to update the current job. Exactly-once paid execution is not promised across lost results.

Role policy is recorded before launch and rechecked before dispatch and after the turn; a configuration change stops the attempt without erasing its raw result. The actual model/thinking observed at turn start is recorded separately. The normal role resolver chooses the mind and pool member. This client checks that the selected model belongs to the recorded policy instead of silently accepting a fallback; it does not select another model or advance profile-pool counters. Usage covers the observed correlated turn, not an invented total for every internal model the ordinary peer environment might run. The peer's native transcript remains its transcript owner.

Around each turn the attempt checks only the retained manifest and comparison (verifyCapturedSource). Full verifyArtifact runs before the planner (which starts every review and resume, so no reviewer reads unverified source), and at additional work and decisions. With eight passes in parallel, a full fsck plus file hashing before and after every turn cost seconds each and contended for the same files.

A change to review execution is first truly exercised by a paid review; scripted fixtures echo the contract exactly. A live validator shard named findings by bare id and fail-fast cancelled its siblings, and a recorded deep policy never reached the peer launch (`--role-profile`), so every deep pass failed its model check. Accept the obvious local form at model-output boundaries, make fixtures model the launch and model selection, and say such changes are unexercised until a live run. When model output is slow or large, check the runtime validator before the prompt: a forced region-per-pass grid, not the planner prompt, made 18-pass reviews.

An attempt is cancelled only when the stop caused its failure (the abort reason or an AbortError). Passes run in parallel, and one pass failing stops its siblings; a sibling that had already failed on its own (for example a policy change after its turn) stays failed rather than being relabelled cancelled.

From the reviewer's start command through its turn (`watchedTurn` wraps start, set_thinking, the policy checks and run_turn, since a peer can hang before its first hello as well), `watchSilence` checks every 30 seconds the reviewer's `sessions.updated_at` (moved by run-state, tool and facts reports, not by heartbeats); after `REVIEWER_SILENCE_MS` (15 minutes) without a change it closes the attempt's program, which cancels the turn and discards the peer (SIGTERM, then SIGKILL in the runtime), and the attempt fails with a retryable error.
Why: nothing else bounds a turn: a stalled provider stream or a hung reviewer process left the review, and every `read_review` waiting on it, running forever (reported by a user on a Claude bridge). The operator chose a generous limit; across 276 completed reviewer turns recorded on 2026-11-05 the longest gap between recorded activity was 9.0 minutes (99th percentile 4.4). A single model call or tool call longer than 15 minutes is stopped too. `specs/review-wait.tla` checks that the wait ends with both the watchdog and kill escalation, and fails without either.
