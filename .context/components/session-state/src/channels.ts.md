## symbol:syncChannels

The runtime reconciles channels at startup, hello, manager/role/name changes, transfer, and retirement. `team:<manager-id>` retains manager/direct-report membership. `organization:<head-id>` anchors a full-branch channel to its original head: current independent heads create one, existing channels keep following that head after a merge, and splitting reuses the channel when present. Threads retain their creation metadata and messages; no history is copied into another channel. Standing channel IDs retain their identity; topic channels record their type, purpose and branch head independently of a display name.
Why: a channel's creator remains the original branch head after a merge. Readers derive its current organization separately; replacing its ID with today's root would lose the branch conversation that a later split must reuse.
Membership is derived from the reporting tree, except explicit invitations, which survive a move. Sleeping people remain members. Derived whole-tree/topic access excludes temporary identities (including durable discarded/observed evidence). Active temporary people can retain an explicit invitation; retired/permanently departed people lose all current access. Keep hard revocation separate from absence of derived scope. Leaving the organization removes membership but preserves the thread's messages.
Why: readers are query-only, and moving teams must stop access to the old team's channel immediately; rebuilding all participants would also reset read cursors or erase cross-team invitations.
Topic scope readers receive access rows with following=false; creator/self-join and invitations manage following separately. The operator's access/following row survives reconciliation without a self-join grant. Audience changes go through `participateIn`, including reconciliation at startup without an outer transaction. Revoking current channel access records a departure rather than erasing the earlier audience; explicit invitations that retain access produce no leave/rejoin pair.
Why: communication history must remain stable after ordinary team moves while former members immediately lose posting access.
